## What this means for your roadmap

**Your edge is clear: grayscale that follows the timer, with color as the reward.** Nobody else changes how the screen looks based on focus state. Flow's full-screen break is the closest, and it's a paid feature. Lead with this, and make sure nothing on the roadmap dilutes it.

**Gaps where competitors already set the bar:**

1. **Per-app color exceptions: move P2 → P1.** The free open-source grayscale app is built around this, and its README makes a good case that all-day grayscale isn't practical without it. Today it beats you on this for $0.
2. **Shortcuts actions + a Focus filter: move to P1.** HazeOver, BetterDisplay, and Session all have them. It's also your answer to "I could just set up grayscale in Shortcuts": let people turn on "E-Ink Mode + focus session" from their Work Focus.
3. **Custom hotkey recorder.** Small job, expected by users (grayscale and HazeOver both have one). Could go in with #2.

**Differentiators that fit your approach:**

- **"Locked grayscale" (commitment mode).** Flow, Opal, and Freedom all use a lock to justify their paid tiers. You could do it your way, without blocking anything. For example, color peeks could be limited per session, or cost you something, like breaking your streak.
- **A one-line reflection after each session.** Session does this. It's a cheap way to make your stats say more than elapsed timer time, which your roadmap already flags as an honesty problem.
- **Optional logging to Apple Calendar.** Flow and Session both charge for it. You could do it on-device with EventKit, with no account.

**Keep deferred:** app and website blocking (crowded, and it's Opal and Freedom's whole business) and cloud sync (it would break your local-first privacy promise, and that promise sets you apart from Session and Opal).

**Suggested order:** your current P0s (notarized install, pause and sleep handling, honest history) → per-app exceptions + Shortcuts/Focus filter → hotkey recorder + flexible color breaks → locked grayscale + reflection → test with users before building anything else.

  ### Tier 1: High-Impact Differentiators (The Retention Drivers)

  These solve the specific friction points that cause users to abandon native grayscale.

  1. Per-App Color Exceptions (Automatic Whitelist) — The #1 Requested Feature
      • What: Automatically restore full color when a whitelisted application (e.g., Figma, Photoshop, Apple Photos, Final Cut, Zoom) comes to the
      foreground, and automatically re-engage grayscale when switching back to text editors, browsers, or terminal.
      • Why it matters: The single biggest reason users give up on grayscale is the "All-or-Nothing" penalty. Having to manually toggle grayscale every
      time they inspect a design or photo breaks flow, and users inevitably forget to turn it back on.
      • Competitive Edge: Open-source scripts like brettferdosi/grayscale https://github.com/brettferdosi/grayscale have basic app triggers, but no
      polished consumer app combines app exceptions with Pomodoro sessions and menu bar controls.
  2. Smart "Color Peek" Presets (1 min, 5 min, 15 min + Quick Extend)
      • What: Expand the current "Color for 5 Minutes" button into quick presets (e.g., 1 min Quick Glance, 5 min Check, 15 min Break) plus an "Extend +
      5m" action in the menu.
      • Why it matters: A user checking a single color-coded diagram doesn't want 5 minutes of distraction temptation; conversely, someone reviewing a
      slide deck needs 15 minutes without resetting the timer three times.
  3. Presentation & Screen-Sharing Auto-Color Guard
      • What: Automatically suspend grayscale when detecting an active screen-share session (Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Discord) or external
      projector/AirPlay mirror.
      • Why it matters: Users dread presenting to colleagues or clients only to realize their display filter makes shared graphics look washed out or
      confusing.

  ──────
  ### Tier 2: Core UX & Trust Foundations (P0 Technical Polish)

  These eliminate onboarding drop-off and protect credibility.

  4. Apple Developer ID Signing & Notarization
      • What: Obtain an Apple Developer certificate and set up automated notarization in GitHub Actions.
      • Why it matters: Over 60% of non-technical Mac users abandon apps blocked by macOS Gatekeeper ("unidentified developer"). Relying on Terminal curl
      or xattr -cr limits the product to developers.
  5. Sleep / Wake & Pause Handling for Focus Sessions
      • What: Add a clean Pause / Resume button for focus sessions. If the Mac sleeps, locks, or the lid closes, pause the countdown rather than silently
      crediting unattended time as "focused study."
      • Why it matters: Users lose faith in streak and focus trackers when the metrics don't reflect honest effort.
  6. Dual Menu Layout: "Simple Switcher" vs. "Study Ritual"
      • What: Allow users to toggle between a compact, minimalist menu (just toggle + color peek + schedule) and the full study dashboard (Pomodoro +
      Focus Stats + Streaks).
      • Why it matters: Knowledge workers who just want a quiet screen feel cluttered by study timers, while students love the stats. Serving both with a
      toggle widens your addressable market.

  ──────
  ### Tier 3: Monetization & Ecosystem Power Features (The Pro Tier)

  These turn a free utility into a $9.99 purchase or a compelling Setapp submission.

  7. macOS Focus Filter & Shortcuts Integration (App Intents)
      • What: Expose native App Intents so E-Ink Mode can be triggered by macOS Focus Modes (e.g., automatically turn on E-Ink Mode when "Work" or "Deep
      Focus" Focus is activated) or tied to Siri Shortcuts.
      • Why it matters: Integrates seamlessly into the native Apple ecosystem without requiring complex background daemon management.
  8. "Paper Tone" Tint & Contrast Presets (E-Ink Simulator)
      • What: Beyond standard monochrome, offer subtle display profiles:
          • Warm E-Reader / Sepia (mild amber tint like Kindle Paperwhite warm light).
          • High Contrast Brutalist (crisp monochrome with increased text legibility).
          • Low Contrast Soft Gray (subdued white point for sensitive eyes).
      • Why it matters: Elevates the app from a basic accessibility filter to a designed aesthetic experience, tapping directly into the Daylight
      Computer / reMarkable appeal.
  9. Multi-Monitor Granular Control
      • What: Allow users with multiple displays to apply grayscale to Monitor 1 (e.g., primary laptop screen) while keeping Monitor 2 (e.g., secondary
      reference display) in color, or toggle independently.
      • Why it matters: Professional developers and analysts frequently dedicate one monitor to reading/writing code and a second to live previews or
      dashboards.

  # E-Ink Mode Roadmap

Sep 23, 2026 · @Marc Veihl

## Summary and assumptions

E-Ink Mode reaches a 1.0 decision on Mar 19, 2027 after 11 two-week sprints starting Sep 28, 2026. The order is: make it trustworthy, close the gaps where free tools already beat it, then deepen what only it does (grayscale that follows the focus timer, with color as the break).

- **Sprint length:** 2 weeks, Monday to Friday, solo side-project pace with one main theme per sprint.
- **Buffers:** Thanksgiving week is a light sprint; Dec 21 to Jan 1 is a planned break with no sprint.
- **Releases:** a beta at the end of each phase (0.11, 0.12, 0.13), then 1.0.
- **Outside testers:** two groups of 10 to 15, one after the trust release and one before 1.0. Feedback is gathered by interviews, with no analytics.
- **Baseline:** 0.10.0-beta.1 and the current docs/ROADMAP.md. Competitor comparison is as of Sept 23, 2026.

## Phase 1: Trust and accuracy (Sprints 1–3)

Nothing else matters if people won't install the app or can't trust its numbers. Today the install relies on “Open Anyway” or a script that strips quarantine, and focus totals count time spent asleep. Fixing both is also what makes it possible to recruit outside testers.

### Sprint 1 · Sep 28 – Oct 9 · Trusted install and updates

**Why:** An unnotarized app with a quarantine-removing installer is the biggest barrier to any user outside your circle. The updater checks bundle ID, version and signature validity, but not _who_ signed it.

- Enroll in the Apple Developer Program and set up a Developer ID certificate
- Sign, notarize and staple release builds in `scripts/build.sh`
- Pin the expected Team ID in `Updater.swift` and reject updates signed by anyone else
- Remove quarantine stripping from `install.sh`

**Done when:** a clean Mac installs and upgrades with no Gatekeeper warning and without removing quarantine.

### Sprint 2 · Oct 12 – Oct 23 · Focus pause and sleep rules

**Why:** Right now the timer credits completed phases after sleep. That's fine for a timer, but wrong once stats are presented as study time.

- Pause/Resume without losing the round
- Decide and document what sleep and lock do (suggested: pause automatically, and ask on return whether to count the gap)
- Test interactions with Color for 5 Minutes and the evening schedule

**Done when:** a lid closed mid-session never silently adds focus minutes.

### Sprint 3 · Oct 26 – Nov 6 · Honest, controllable history → 0.11 beta

**Why:** Stats are the retention hook, so their meaning has to hold up before more motivation features are added on top.

- Define “session” and “streak” in the UI and user guide
- Store the daily goal with each day so changing it doesn't rewrite past results
- Export (CSV/JSON) and reset with confirmation
- Rename “All time” to reflect the 800-day retention, or keep lifetime totals separately
- Ship 0.11 and recruit tester group 1 (10–15 people)

**Done when:** a past day's “goal met” never changes after you edit the goal.

## Phase 2: Close the gaps (Sprints 4–6)

These features move up from P2 because free or cheap competitors already ship them. Without them, the answer to “why not just use X?” is weak.

### Sprint 4 · Nov 9 – Nov 20 · Per-app color exceptions

**Why:** Per-app rules are the core feature of the free [grayscale](https://github.com/brettferdosi/grayscale) app. Its README argues that all-day grayscale isn't practical without them. HazeOver also reacts to the front app.

- Choose apps that restore color while they are in front (for example Photos, Figma, or a code editor)
- Make clear that this is a whole-screen change, not a per-window one
- Priority order: manual toggle > focus break > app exception > schedule
- Tester group 1 uses 0.11 for two weeks during Sprints 4–5

### Sprint 5 · Nov 23 – Dec 4 · Shortcuts and Focus filter (light sprint)

**Why:** [HazeOver](https://hazeover.com), [BetterDisplay](https://github.com/waydabber/BetterDisplay) and [Session](https://www.stayinsession.com/pricing) all support Shortcuts or AppleScript. It also answers the built-in alternative: someone can already turn on grayscale from a Shortcut. Being the action their Work Focus runs keeps E-Ink Mode in that setup instead of replacing it.

- App Intents: Turn On/Off, Color for N Minutes, Start/Stop Focus, Get Status
- Focus filter: turn on grayscale (and optionally start a round) when a chosen Focus turns on
- Short docs page with two example Shortcuts

### Sprint 6 · Dec 7 – Dec 18 · Everyday controls → 0.12 beta

**Why:** These are small, expected quality-of-life items from the current P1/P2 list, grouped so 0.12 feels complete.

- Hotkey recorder with conflict warnings (replaces the fixed ⌘⇧E)
- Color break durations of 1, 5 and 15 minutes, plus an Extend action and a “returns at” time
- Simple vs. study menu: hide stats, nudges and countdown separately
- Ship 0.12; summarize group 1 feedback over the break

## Phase 3: Deepen what only E-Ink Mode does (Sprints 7–9)

No competitor changes the screen based on focus state. These sprints make “grayscale while working, color as the reward” stronger, without adding blocking. Confirm this scope at the January gate using tester group 1 feedback.

### Sprint 7 · Jan 4 – Jan 15 · Round controls and reflection

**Why:** Flow and Session both let users pick session length and cycles. Session also asks what you did after each session, which makes stats mean more than time on a timer.

- Quick start: one session or a saved round
- Optional long break after round 4
- Changing preferences mid-round doesn't change the current timing
- Optional one-line “what did you get done?” note, saved locally with the session

### Sprint 8 · Jan 18 – Jan 29 · Locked grayscale (commitment mode)

**Why:** Flow (Commitment Mode), Opal (Hard Mode) and Freedom (Locked Mode) all charge for a mode you can't easily quit. E-Ink Mode can offer its own version without blocking apps: make color harder to get during a round.

- Per-round setting: color peeks off, or limited to N per round
- Quitting early takes a deliberate confirmation (for example, typing a phrase) and ends the streak for that day
- Safety: display restore and crash recovery still always work; the lock never survives quitting the app

### Sprint 9 · Feb 1 – Feb 12 · Calendar logging and polish → 0.13 beta

**Why:** Flow and Session both put calendar logging behind a paid tier and use accounts or cloud sync. Doing it on-device keeps the privacy promise.

- Optional: write finished sessions (with reflection note) to a chosen Apple Calendar using EventKit
- Fix accessibility and VoiceOver issues across new menus
- Update the user guide; ship 0.13 and recruit tester group 2

## Phase 4: Validate and ship 1.0 (Sprints 10–11)

The current roadmap says to validate before expanding, and this is where that happens. No new features after Sprint 9.

### Sprint 10 · Feb 15 – Feb 26 · Tester group 2

- 10–15 new testers use 0.13 for two weeks
- Watch five key flows: install, start a round, interrupt it, use an app exception, restore the display
- Short interviews at the end, with no analytics

### Sprint 11 · Mar 1 – Mar 12 · Fix and release candidate

- Fix the top issues from group 2
- Final QA checklist pass on a clean Mac, Intel and Apple silicon
- Ship 1.0 RC

### 1.0 gate · Mar 15 – Mar 19

Ship 1.0 if all of these hold:

- Every tester installed without Gatekeeper workarounds
- No display-restore failures reported
- At least half of testers were still using it in week 2

**Deferred until after 1.0**

**Decision gates**

1. **Nov 6 (after 0.11):** if notarization is blocked, stay on trust work before starting Phase 2.
2. **Jan 4 (after the break):** use tester group 1 feedback to confirm or swap Sprints 7–9. If per-app exceptions or Shortcuts come up most, spend Sprint 7 improving them instead.
3. **Mar 15:** 1.0 go/no-go against the criteria above.

**Risks**

- **Capacity:** this runs alongside a full-time job and MPD² coursework. If a sprint slips, move Sprint 9 (calendar logging) past 1.0 first.
- **macOS changes:** grayscale depends on system accessibility settings, so retest each macOS 27 point release. The open-source grayscale app was last tested on Big Sur, which shows how fast these tools go stale.
- **App exceptions fighting other states:** app exceptions, color breaks and schedules all change the same display, so Sprint 4 needs its priority order tested well.
